# Hard Filter Results

## Filter Scope

Nine mobile visual candidates were tested against product truth, Law of One, interaction silhouettes, Space hierarchy, prior rejection families, platform containment, and learner/reviewer separation.

## Rejected Candidates

- sbv-04: paper/notebook ancestry and ornamental page motion.
- sbv-05: toy-like poster grid; counters and signals compete with the current task.
- sbv-06: Space-first journey reverses the required Learning priority.
- sbv-07: explicit monochrome wireframe appearance and weak state separation.
- sbv-08: glass/aurora/pill recurrence with contrast and platform-parity risk.

## Surviving Candidates

- sbv-01: passes task priority, control clarity, containment, and platform structure.
- sbv-02: passes task clarity and responsive mapping, despite visual coldness.
- sbv-03: passes consumer-app completion, hierarchy, and multi-device composition.
- sbv-09: passes all hard filters and adds independent stable-brand/current-hall color roles.

## Product Truth Violations

sbv-06 makes browsing prerequisite to Learning. sbv-05 makes status modules feel like the product core. sbv-04 flattens Space into notebook navigation.

## Layout Or Proof Violations

sbv-05 cannot preserve narrow-phone containment; sbv-08 risks blurred contrast and Android drift; sbv-07 creates nested outline noise. During rendered refinement, sbv-09 initially had a tablet split too narrow and a Space grid item extending beyond its parent; both were corrected before promotion.

## Notes For Mutation

Separate stable brand from current-hall color, keep white task surfaces fully opaque, fit the phone primary action above the floating navigation, use a broad single-column tablet task, and prevent grid intrinsic sizing from expanding Space holders.
