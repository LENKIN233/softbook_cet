# Hard Filter Results

## Filter Scope

All eight directions were checked against single-card focus, Law of One, canonical interaction silhouette, Space hierarchy, one-screen containment, accessibility, and the requirement to beat the rejected glass baseline rather than recolor it.

## Rejected Candidates

- `mue-04`: retains the same frosted panels, capsule grammar, and generic app personality as the rejected baseline.
- `mue-05`: promotes “today”, counts, and resume state above the current CET card.
- `mue-06`: makes coral a full-screen decorative field, reducing text stability and editorial restraint.
- `mue-07`: turns the system-sequenced flow into a visible management timeline.
- `mue-08`: repeats the shipped failure by compressing the card into the top third and treating empty atmosphere as composition.

## Surviving Candidates

- `mue-01`: large editorial card with a library margin rail and an operation attached to the same object.
- `mue-02`: physical index stack with clear 2×2 multiple-choice silhouette.
- `mue-03`: efficient answer rail with high task clarity and a distinct dark-plum environment.

## Product Truth Violations

`mue-05` and `mue-07` obscure the system-led current card. `mue-08` violates the focal scale required by the flip silhouette. `mue-04` is not a product-truth violation by itself, but it fails the explicit objective to stop looking like a generic glass learning app.

## Layout Or Proof Violations

`mue-06` risks contrast failure when coral becomes the text environment. `mue-08` creates a dead lower viewport and weak thumb-to-task continuity. The three survivors render at 393 × 852 with the primary operation clear of the floating navigation.

## Notes For Mutation

Use `mue-01` as the base; borrow `mue-02`'s nested physical compartments for Space; borrow `mue-03`'s lower-density answer rail only for auto-scored interactions. Reduce decorative glass to chrome and retain paper thickness through grain, rim, and shadow.
